Agentic Ops Digest — September 7, 2026: The Governance Arms Race Is Here

Sep 7, 2026

Agents are moving faster than controls. This week’s headline is clear: organizations can’t govern AI they don’t even know they’re running. CIO.com flagged this as the #1 pain point for Sep 2026, and Security Boulevard confirmed last week that most enterprises still struggle to detect and contain out-of-scope agents. If you’re building or deploying agents without governance baked in at runtime, you’re not shipping software — you’re shipping risk.

Here’s what matters this week:

1. You Can’t Govern What You Don’t Know You’re Running (CIO.com, Sep 1)

A fresh piece on the governance visibility gap calls out the core problem: organizations are rolling out agents without centralized discovery. Every department ships their own. Shadow agents proliferate. No inventory, no policy enforcement surface, no way to block bad behavior before it scales.

Heed angle: This is exactly why plugin-enforced governance exists. Heed doesn’t ask teams to maintain inventories or fill forms — it intercepts every tool call at the agent loop level and enforces policies regardless of how the agent was spawned. Discovery is the human layer; enforcement is ours. ShlexClassifier deploys in seconds, catches risky patterns before execution. That’s governance you get whether you knew about the agent or not.

2. F5 + MuleSoft Go Inline on Agent Fabric Governance (Yahoo Finance / F5, Sep 2)

F5 and Salesforce’s MuleSoft announced an integration that delivers “inline security and governance for Agent Fabric and Agentic AI applications.” This moves the paradigm from bolt-on gateways to inline interception — same architecture pattern that underlies Heed’s approach. It’s the biggest enterprise vendor signal yet that governance belongs inside the agent loop, not after it.

Heed angle: Enterprise tools like this target large orgs with complex integration layers. They won’t be practical for individual developers, consultants, or small teams building autonomous agents. Heed fills that gap — lightweight, open-core, designed for the person who writes code and needs it to behave. Same philosophy (inline enforcement), different scale. Our free tier covers single-agent deployment; enforce mode is live.

3. Broadcom Bakes Agent Governance Into Private Cloud, Not as Bolt-On (forkast.news, Sep 2)

Broadcom’s move to bake governance directly into VMware Aria rather than offering it as a separate add-on reinforces the industry trend: governance can’t survive as a retrofitted checkbox. It has to be structural. The article notes that customers are demanding native capability, not another portal to log into during an incident.

Heed angle: This is our thesis statement. Governance embedded in the runtime, not layered on top. Rule analyzer CLI makes adding rules trivial.

4. Zenity Raises $125M Series C for Agent Security (Pulse 2.0, Aug 3)

Zenity closed a massive Series C round focused exclusively on agent identity and intent monitoring. The funding signals venture conviction that agent security is a standalone category — not a feature of existing CASB or EDR vendors. Their focus: detecting when agents deviate from intended behavior patterns.

5. First 24 Hours of an AI Agent Security Incident (CSO Online, Aug 27)

A deep dive on the early response window for agent incidents reveals a painful reality: most orgs didn’t realize an agent had breached until hours later. The delay comes from poor observability and the absence of pre-execution checkpoints. By the time someone notices an agent writing files it shouldn’t, the damage is done.
