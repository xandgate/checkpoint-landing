Agentic Ops Digest — November 23, 2026

Nov 23, 2026
Agentic Ops Digest is a weekly curation of developments at the intersection of AI agent security, governance, and regulatory compliance.

The Headline

OpenAI’s Chief Scientist proposes perplexity-based development limits — Jakub Pachocki published a landmark call for the industry to jointly establish shared metrics and “common limits” on frontier model development, tied directly to measurable intelligence milestones.1

This isn’t just about model benchmarks. It’s an explicit proposal for pacing mechanisms — the exact kind of structured oversight that plugin-enforced governance layers like Heed operationalize in production today.

What Matters This Week

1. OpenAI Proposes Perplexity as a Shared Safety Metric1

Pachocki argues that cross-entropy loss (log-perplexity) has been the single most reliable predictor of emergent capabilities since GPT-3. He shows OpenAI reaching below 0.8 log-perplexity — correlating with transformative capabilities in cybersecurity, mathematics, and deep learning research. His proposal:

  • Establish perplexity as a shared transparency benchmark across labs
  • Define common limits developers commit to not crossing until monitoring advances reach a concrete milestone
  • Treat the next ~0.68–0.7 window as potentially bringing entirely new, currently-untested capabilities

Heed angle: Pachocki explicitly calls for “common limits that developers are committed to not crossing until our ability to understand and monitor AI behavior achieves a certain concrete milestone.” That is what Heed does at the harness level — enforcing hard boundaries on model behavior, regardless of how capable the underlying model becomes. As model intelligence compounds, manual oversight breaks down. Plugin-enforced gates don’t.

2. OpenRouter Offboards Providers After Data Policy Violations2

Security researchers (via Dragos) found that several inference providers were failing to uphold their data retention promises. OpenRouter’s response, published Nov 22:

  • Offboarded 3 providers entirely
  • Removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility
  • Notified all potentially affected customers
  • Conducting network path verification to detect request forwarding to third parties

Affected providers include Atlas Cloud, Novita, Phala, and Together — all posting incident reports.

Heed angle: This is the data-processing equivalent of an uncontrolled tool-call chain. When inference providers promise “zero data retention” and fail to deliver, downstream agents and users trust compromised boundaries. Heed’s plugin architecture — especially critical_denylist and harness-level hooks — exists precisely to enforce policy boundaries before they break. The lesson: trust your infrastructure, verify every hop.

3. Show HN: Subagent Governance Game3

A developer built an interactive game where you play as an LLM’s subagent — navigating task delegation, approval workflows, and conflict resolution between parallel subagents. 313 points on HN, reflecting genuine interest in the coordination problems.

Heed angle: The game exposes real problems we face: when multiple subagents run concurrently, who governs the aggregate output? How do you prevent one subagent from exploiting a weaker harness than another? Heed’s before_tool_call hooks + requireApproval work across the full agent graph — not just the primary agent but every delegated child. Governance scales with complexity.

Context to Watch

The EU AI Act’s Art. 14 (human oversight) and Art. 71 (post-market monitoring) requirements are coming into sharper focus. New entrants like Pandex.io are building APIs specifically designed for AI agents to access regulated legal text across 8 European jurisdictions — signaling that the regulatory layer itself is becoming machine-readable and agent-facing. This creates a new category of compliance requirement: agents must not only follow human-defined rules, but also navigate machine-readable regulatory constraints in real time.

Meanwhile, Anthropic’s power infrastructure commitments reveal that gas and coal will provide 87% of their projected 2028 compute needs, raising questions about whether sustainability commitments can keep pace with capability growth.

Bottom Line

Three signals point in one direction: as agent capabilities compound, governance can no longer be ad hoc. OpenAI is proposing shared metrics because manual oversight won’t scale. OpenRouter had to respond to provider failures because infrastructure trust was assumed rather than enforced. And the EU is making regulation machine-readable because human-reviewed compliance can’t keep up with agent speed.

Plugin-enforced governance isn’t a premium feature anymore — it’s infrastructure.
