4. HITL Oversight Must Be Policy-Driven, Not Reactive

Source: Redis/Strata, May 11, 2026
https://redis.io/blog/ai-human-in-the-loop/
https://www.strata.io/blog/agentic-identity/practicing-the-human-in-the-loop/

New architectural patterns for human-in-the-loop (HITL) emphasize that oversight must be dynamic, policy-driven, and identity-enforced at the agent level—not bolted on after decisions. Research warns that HITL is not a cure-all: without clear role definition, metrics, and auditable principles, it compounds risk rather than mitigating it.

Why it matters for Heed: Shadow mode and approval gates are governance primitives, not fire extinguishers. Heed users can enforce patterns like “flag autonomous actions >$10K for approval” without changing code.

5. Graph-of-Agents Framework Optimizes Multi-Agent Routing

Source: arXiv, April 2026
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17148

A new academic framework (Graph-of-Agents) models multi-agent collaboration as selective node routing—agents are sampled intelligently based on domain specialization and task fitness rather than invoked in sequence. This reduces latency and hallucination loops in complex agent graphs.

Why it matters for Heed: As agents fan out and coordinate, governance becomes graph-aware. Heed’s plugin layer must track cross-agent dependencies and enforce policies at orchestration boundaries, not just individual actions.
